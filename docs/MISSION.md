# Mission, Vision, Values

The top of the pyramid: every feature brief's "Why" should trace to this
document. Doctrine lives in the repo `CLAUDE.md` hard rules; this page states
what all of it is *for*.

## Mission

**Make the US dollar system legible: where federal money comes from, where
every dollar goes, how the Treasury's and the Fed's plumbing works, and how it
transmits to markets and the rates people pay — built from the primary record,
readable by anyone, and cited down to every number.**

## Vision

The record of the dollar is public but illegible. FRED holds nearly every
series but assumes you know which to ask for. USASpending stops at outlays.
The Fed's releases are written for professionals. Auction results are public
but their meaning lives in paywalled press. Journalism asserts the chain —
*deficits → yields → your mortgage* — without ever showing the machine.

Buck is the public's instrument for understanding that machine. One
trustworthy place where a reader can:

- **check the system's state in a minute** — the debt, the government's cash
  balance, the latest auction, each number dated and sourced;
- **understand the machine in an hour** — a readable follow-the-dollar
  narrative, not a wall of dashboards;
- **explore for themselves** — instruments over the primary data, deep-linked
  and shareable;
- **and never take our word for anything** — every claim cited to the agency
  of record, every interpretation attributed to whoever holds it.

## Values

1. **Trust is the product.** A reader who catches us once being
   wrong-and-silent loses the site forever.
2. **Objectivity is load-bearing.** Mechanics are facts with citations;
   interpretations are attributed, never asserted in Buck's voice. No
   villains, no imputed motives. Every sentence should survive being read
   aloud by any party it describes.
3. **Honesty about what we don't know.** Missing data is a gap, never a zero.
   Provisional says provisional. Where sources disagree, both numbers appear.
   Where economists disagree, the disagreement is the content.
4. **Plain language.** Complex things explained simply and precisely — the
   Money Stuff register. Never make a number wrong to make it friendly.
5. **The reader's independence.** The goal is not that readers believe us —
   it's that they can verify us. Sources, methods, and code are open.

## What Buck is not

Not an advocacy site — we don't score fiscal policy, we explain its
mechanics. Not a news site — we cover the record, not the discourse. Not
investment advice — ever. Not affiliated with the Treasury, the Federal
Reserve, or any agency — independence is load-bearing. Not a forecasting
shop — if a projection ever appears, it is someone else's, attributed
(CBO's, the Fed's), never ours.
